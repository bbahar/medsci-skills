Main-text word limits count **only the Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion**. Over-limit submissions are returned to the author without review. Title limit 120 characters including spaces (Blood Commentary 60).

- **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses** are submitted as Regular Articles.
- **Brief Reports** document definitive results, not incomplete or preliminary findings; review is equally rigorous and the acceptance rate is lower than for Regular Articles.
- **Single-case reports and small case series** are considered only if they elucidate very novel and important disease biology or therapy.
- **Industry restriction**: pharmaceutical or medical-device company employees, and medical writers supported by such companies, may have no role in writing Review, Perspective, How I Treat or Blood Spotlight articles; for Special Reports, company employees cannot be authors and company-supported writers may have no role. Disclosed editing services for non-native English speakers are permitted.
- **Plenary Papers**: the Editors may designate definitive original research of exceptional importance.
- The title page states text and abstract word counts, figure/table count and reference count; supplemental data cannot be used to get around word or figure limits.

## Abstract Requirements

Unstructured — a **continuous narrative with no subheadings and no references**; ≤ 250 words (≤ 200 for Brief Reports). Plain language for a broad readership, minimal jargon and acronyms. Preferably contains (per ICMJE):

```
Context/background (1–2 plain-language sentences for readers new to the topic)
Purpose: explicit objectives, not general statements
Methods: participants, settings, measurements, analytical methods
Main findings: specific effect sizes with statistical and clinical significance
Main conclusions: new and important aspects
```

**Key Points** (Regular Articles and Brief Reports): 1–2 bullet statements, **≤ 140 characters each including spaces**, plain language, placed immediately before the Abstract in both the manuscript file and the submission metadata. Published freely online and in print; not indexed by PubMed. The guidelines say Key Points are "required upon manuscript submission" in one place and "must be added at the revision stage" in another [VERIFY] — include them at first submission to be safe.

## Required Sections (Original Article)

Single manuscript file with page numbers in the header or footer, in this order:

1. **Title page** — title ≤ 120 characters including spaces, active voice, **no punctuation or abbreviations** (common abbreviations allowed only if defined in the abstract); species named in the title if only non-human models were used. Running head ≤ 50 characters; full author names; numbered affiliations; corresponding author's address, e-mail, phone and fax; word counts (text and abstract), figure/table count, reference count; scientific category (Regular Articles, Brief Reports); **Data Sharing Statement** subsection (required for all original research — where deposited data are and whom to contact for the rest; clinical trials must address sharing of deidentified individual participant data per ICMJE, including a refusal if data will not be shared).
2. **Key Points**
3. **Abstract**
4. **Introduction**
5. **Methods** — enough detail on all major experimental plans and procedures. Human subjects: IRB/ethics approval and written informed consent; who analysed the data and confirmation that all authors had access to primary clinical-trial data; trial registration number and registry. Phase 3 RCTs: CONSORT flow diagram and checklist content in the body (supplement only if length forces it). Parenteral or high-intensity regimens: a separate supplemental file with drug names, dose and modifications, route, diluent and rate, cycle length/number, premedications and supportive care, and monitoring. High-throughput data (mRNA, miRNA, proteomic, genomic arrays): deposited in GEO, ArrayExpress or an open repository, with accession number or link active at submission. Animal studies: ARRIVE guidelines — protocol approval, procedure details, bias minimisation (randomisation, blinding), animal descriptors with RRIDs, controls, and sample-size justification.
6. **Results**
7. **Discussion** (combined with Results in Brief Reports)
8. **Acknowledgments** — all support; corporate funding note ("This study was supported (in part) by research funding from [company] to [initials]"); grant names and numbers; non-author writers/editors with role and funding source.
9. **Authorship Contributions** and **Conflict of Interest Disclosures** — each author's contribution category and potential conflicts; "no competing financial interests" must be stated explicitly; in both the metadata and the manuscript text.
10. **References**
11. **Tables**
12. **Figure Legends**
13. **Figures**

**Style**: no footnotes (use parentheses sparingly); define abbreviations at first mention, no nonstandard abbreviations, and do not abbreviate terms used fewer than three times; give a compound's chemical name after the first use of its common name; abbreviate units only with numbers. See the Blood Style Guide.

**References**: numbered in order of citation, cited as superscript numbers, formatted per the Blood Style Guide; First Edition (prepublished) citations include the DOI, and PMIDs can be added to link prepublished articles.

## Statistical Reporting

The manuscript-preparation guidelines do not have a statistics section. Stated:

- Abstract findings give **specific effect sizes and their statistical and clinical significance** (ICMJE).
- Animal studies describe how sample-size adequacy was determined and steps to minimise bias.
- Phase 3 RCTs follow CONSORT.

Defaults, not stated [VERIFY]:
- Exact p-values to 2–3 significant figures; p < 0.001 below that.
- 95% CI for primary outcomes.
- Name the statistical software and version.

## Figures

- **Maximum**: 7 figures for Regular Articles; 3 figures or tables for Brief Reports; 2 for Letters and Blood Spotlights; see the table above. Digital images are required.
- **Resolution**: Low-resolution acceptable for review (the review PDF must not exceed 5 MB); high-resolution figures required after acceptance (see the Blood digital artwork guidelines).
- **Format**: GIF, JPEG, PDF, TIFF or EPS; PowerPoint accepted but strongly discouraged. Figure numbers in Arabic numerals on the images.
- **Color**: Not stated [VERIFY].
- **Image integrity**: no feature may be enhanced, obscured, moved, removed or introduced; composites from different gels, fields or exposures need black dividing lines and an explanation in the legend; whole-image brightness/contrast/colour adjustments only; nonlinear (gamma) adjustments disclosed. All images in accepted papers are screened; unexplained manipulation holds First Edition publication and can revoke acceptance.
- **Legends**: begin with a short **bold** summary sentence; for images give microscope, objectives (type, magnification, numerical aperture), temperature, imaging medium, fluorochromes, camera, acquisition and processing software and operations.
- **Tables**: brief descriptive title; at most ~8 columns so each fits one US Letter page; numbered and cited in order.
- **Supplement**: one Word file (Methods, Tables, Figures, video legends, Appendices), labelled "Supplemental Table 1" / "Supplemental Figure 1" / "Video 1"; videos .mov/.avi/.wmv under 5 MB, ~30–45 s. Fee of $105 per standard data supplement on acceptance, plus $105 if over 5 MB (waived for Review Articles, How I Treat, Perspectives).

## Common Rejection Reasons

Derived from the journal's own author guidelines (not from editor-decision data):

1. Over the article-type limits for words, figures/tables or references — returned without review.
2. Single-case reports or small case series without very novel disease biology or therapy, or Brief Reports presenting preliminary findings.
3. Methods moved to the supplement, or a supplement used to get around word or figure limits.
4. Missing Data Sharing Statement, or for a clinical trial, no statement on sharing individual participant data.
5. Image manipulation that is not disclosed or that alters the data — acceptance can be revoked.
6. High-throughput data not deposited with an accession number active at submission.

## Positioning

**Choose Blood when**: the work is high-impact hematology research for a broad hematology readership, basic or clinical, with findings strong enough to be summarised in 1–2 plain-language Key Points. Transfusion-medicine work belongs here when its significance reaches beyond the transfusion community.

**Avoid Blood for**: work whose audience is mainly transfusion-medicine specialists (Transfusion or Vox Sanguinis fit better); manuscripts that need the supplement to carry the methods; anything over the article-type limits.
