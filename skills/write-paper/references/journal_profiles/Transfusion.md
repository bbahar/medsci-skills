## Manuscript Types and Word Limits

Word limits exclude the Abstract, References and Figure Legends unless noted. Figures and tables share one combined cap.

| Type | Body Word Limit | Abstract | References | Figures/Tables (combined) |
|------|----------------|----------|------------|---------------------------|
| Original Research | 3,500 | 250 (structured) | No limit | 8 |
| Brief Report | 1,800 | 250 | 25 | 3 |
| Case Report | 1,800 | 250 | 25 | 3 |
| Review Article | 4,000 (excluding references, tables, illustrations) | 250 | No limit | 8 |
| Rapid Review | 800–1,200 (title, subheads and Key Ideas not counted) | None — 2–3 bullet Key Ideas instead | 10 | No tables; one concept cartoon encouraged |
| Commentary | 4,000 | None | No limit | 8 |
| Clinical Research Focus (methodology; ≤ 4 authors) | 1,500 | 250 | 20 | 1 figure + 1 table |

| Transfusion Medicine Illustrated | 250 | None | No limit | 3 visuals |
| Report of New Alleles or Antigens (online only) | 600 | None | 5 | 1 |
| Letter to the Editor | Two double-spaced pages (10–12 pt) | None | 5 | 2 |

- **Rapid Review**, **Commentary** and **Clinical Research Focus**: contact the Editor-in-Chief before submitting with questions about the section.
- **How Do I …**: contact the section editor before submitting. Title begins "How Do I (or We) …"; authored or co-authored by a recognised authority; describes creating, implementing or improving a policy, process or program, with data showing why it matters; not a literature review. Prepare as for Original Research.
- **Report of New Alleles or Antigens**: complete title page, no abstract; suggested format Brief Background or Case Report / Brief Methods / Serologic results (table preferred) / Ethnicity, prevalence or allele frequency / Brief summary. Consult SNP databases and give the reference number; an official ISBT allele/antigen designation is provided after peer review.
- **Case Report** sections: Abstract, Case Presentation, Discussion, Conclusion.
- **Letter to the Editor**: closing signature at the end with authors' names, degrees, affiliations and locations; corresponding author's email under their name.

## Abstract Requirements

Original Research (250 words), four sections — note the last heading is **DISCUSSION**, not "Conclusion":

```
BACKGROUND: Summarize the question that you attempted to answer.
STUDY DESIGN AND METHODS: Summarize how you attempted to answer the question.
RESULTS: Summarize your main findings.
DISCUSSION: Summarize the implications of your main findings.
```

Abstract and Key Words go on their own page.

## Required Sections (Original Article)

Single Word file (.doc/.docx), double-spaced (2.0), each part starting on a separate page: Title Page; Abstract and Key Words; Main Text; Acknowledgments; References; Tables (at the end, numbered, captions kept); Appendices; Figure Legends (separate page at the end). Figures are separate files with **no captions on the figures**, labelled FIGURE 1, FIGURE 2, ….

**Title page** (an incomplete title page gets the manuscript returned): concise title with **no abbreviations or brand names**; running headline ≤ 40 characters including spaces, at the bottom of the title page and labelled; authors' first and last names without titles, degrees or rank, with superscript attribution to department, institution and location; corresponding author's name, email and telephone; sources of support (grants, equipment, drugs); **word count**; **number of figures, tables and references**.

1. **Introduction** — purpose, rationale, pertinent references only; no data or conclusions from the work.
2. **Materials and Methods** — the guidelines name this section both "MATERIALS AND METHODS" (section list) and "STUDY DESIGN AND METHODS" (section guidance) [VERIFY which heading the journal uses]. Selection of subjects and controls with numbers per group; methods, equipment, reagents and procedures in reproducible detail; references for established methods; generic drug names, doses and routes. Publishing implies willingness to share materials (cell lines, DNA clones, antibodies) with academic researchers.
   - Human experiments: state conformity with the institution's human-experimentation committee standards; informed consent if a patient is identifiable. Animal experiments: state the institutional or National Research Council guide followed. Recombinant DNA: state containment per NIH guidelines.
   - Statistics (see below).
3. **Results** — logical sequence; cite every table and figure in numerical order.
4. **Discussion** — new and important aspects, implications, limitations, relation to other studies; no unqualified claims, no priority claims; label new hypotheses as such.
5. **Acknowledgments** — non-author contributions, technical help, financial and material support; people named need to have given written permission.

**Style**: active voice where possible; no registered-trademark symbols anywhere; define every abbreviation at first use (except units); metric units (body weight in kg or lb), °C, blood pressure in torr or mmHg. Red cell blood group antigen and allele terminology follows ISBT conventions; platelet antigen nomenclature follows Metcalfe et al., Vox Sang 2003.

**Blood Group Genomics** manuscripts: three-letter amino-acid codes with c./p. notation (e.g. c.574C>T, p.Arg192Trp); cDNA or full-gene sequencing to define a new allele; GenBank/EMBL accession numbers; a separate "Web Resources" section after Acknowledgments listing URLs with access dates (no URLs in the text).

**References**: numbered in order of first mention; superscript Arabic numerals placed after punctuation; list the first six authors then "et al."; page numbers required; references verified against the originals. No "unpublished observations" or "personal communications" in the list (written communications may be cited in parentheses in the text with name and month/year); accepted manuscripts cited as "(in press)".

## Statistical Reporting

Stated in the guidelines:

- Describe methods in enough detail for a knowledgeable reader with the original data to verify the results.
- Quantify findings with indicators of measurement error or uncertainty such as **confidence intervals**; **avoid sole reliance on p values**.
- Discuss eligibility of subjects; give randomization details; describe blinding and its success; report treatment complications, numbers of observations, and losses to observation (e.g. dropouts).
- Cite standard works (with pages) for design and statistical methods where possible.
- **Specify any general-use computer programs used.**
- Tables: asterisks are reserved for levels of probability; linked footnotes use superscript letters a, b, c ….

Default, not stated [VERIFY]: report exact p-values to 2–3 significant figures, p < 0.001 below that.

## Common Rejection Reasons

Derived from the journal's own author guidelines (not from editor-decision data):

1. Incomplete title page — the guidelines state such manuscripts are returned for revision (missing running headline, word count, or counts of figures, tables and references are the easy misses).
2. Over the combined figure-and-table cap (8 for Original Research) or the 3,500-word body limit.
3. Statistics reported as p values alone without confidence intervals or other measures of uncertainty.
4. Methods not reproducible, or unwilling to share materials such as cell lines, clones or antibodies.
5. Abbreviations or brand names in the title, or blood group terminology not following ISBT conventions.

## Positioning

**Choose TRANSFUSION when**: the work is in blood banking, transfusion medicine, or cellular and gene therapies, including blood group genomics (new alleles and antigens have a dedicated short format), practical program and process experience ("How Do I …"), or clinical research methodology. Case reports are accepted.

**Avoid TRANSFUSION for**: studies that need more than eight combined figures and tables in the main text (move backup tables to online supplementary material); work submitted or published elsewhere beyond a meeting abstract of 500 words or fewer.
